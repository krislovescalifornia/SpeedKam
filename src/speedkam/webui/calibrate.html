What's a "road feature"? Any spot on the road you can both see here and physically reach with a tape measure. The easy way: draw big chalk X's on the road, then click those.

Click each point below, then type its real-world X/Y (metres). Add 4 or more, and spread them across both edges of the road — not all on one line, or the maths fails.

Measured points

  1. Pick an origin on the near edge of the road = (0, 0) (e.g. lined up with your driveway).
  2. X = distance along the road (left↔right, the way cars drive). Y = distance across it (near edge → far edge).
  3. Chalk marks along the near curb (X=0,3,6…, Y=0) and the far curb (same X's, Y=road width). Both edges = not collinear.
  4. Click each mark, type its X and Y. Keep them in the middle third of the image — that's the only band cars are timed in.
  5. Lower reprojection error = better. Aim under ~0.3 m.

Two things matter. Use a phone GPS speed app, not your dashboard (it reads high) — the calibration is only as accurate as the speed you type. And drive at a steady speed through the whole view.

Drive-by calibration

No tape measure. Drive past a few times at a known steady speed; SpeedKam turns each pass into calibration points itself (speed × time = distance).

  1. Set your known speed and pick a lane below.
  2. Press Start, then drive past. The pass appears in the list.
  3. Do 2–3 passes per lane. Then switch lane (e.g. the other direction) and do 2–3 more — at least two lanes are required.
  4. Press Build calibration.
